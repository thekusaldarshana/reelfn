# Editorial

Write for the audience in `brand/brand.json` (`editorial.audience`) in the episode's language, in the creator's tone
(`editorial.tone`). Defaults: curious adults and teens; warm, direct and precise.

## Story shape
- Open inside a familiar situation in the first second. Say what the viewer can see before naming the academic topic.
  No title card: the first frame is the situation or the question.
- Let a short, honest question create the turn ("Wait, how could that work?"), and answer it within the same reel.
- Show the mechanism, one idea at a time: concrete example, visible change, then the smallest general rule that explains it.
- Name the concept once the viewer has a reason to care (beat 3 or later; the validator enforces this). Give one
  concrete use. End on a question the viewer can answer in a comment.

## Voice
- Conversational and lightly playful; humour should clarify, never replace a step of reasoning. One earned joke is enough.
- A brief "Okay," "Wait," or "See?" can bridge a change of direction. Avoid catchphrases and filler between steps.
- Choose vivid, physical verbs for what viewers see: split, toss, vanish, flip, catch. Use a technical term when it
  names a useful idea, then ground it in the visible mechanism.
- Keep the writing original; never imitate a specific creator.

## Beats
Each beat needs a visual action that carries an idea; avoid slides of bare text. Keep spoken words plausible for the
seconds allowed (about 2 words per second). Captions and on-screen labels support the narration rather than
repeating every word. For claim scope and numbers read [[science]]; for delivery and timing read [[audio]].
